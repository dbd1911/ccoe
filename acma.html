The Role

What an ACMA Is — and Is Not

Established under the Defense Acquisition Workforce Improvement Act (DAWIA) program and governed by the Army Director, Acquisition Career Management (DACM) policy of 2 February 2022. Read the governing memo itself — it is short and it is the authority.

The Role

Senior Advocate, DACM-Chartered

An ACMA is a command-nominated, DACM-chartered General Officer, SES member, or GS-15/Colonel-level senior leader who serves as the strategic advisor for acquisition career development in their organization — the liaison between the Army DACM Office and the PEO/Commander, and the advocate for every Army Acquisition Workforce (AAW) professional in the formation.

  • Advises senior leadership on acquisition career and leader development, policy, and programs
  • Keeps the organization informed on DAWIA certification status and the annual AAW Standards
  • Champions education, training, and talent management aligned to the AAW Human Capital Strategic Plan
  • Designates a Lead Organizational Acquisition Point of Contact (OAP) as the on-site support to supervisors and AAW personnel
  • Chartered for a term of up to four years, renewable by mutual DACM/command decision
Not the Role

What This Node Doesn't Do

This is a resource center, deliberately. It carries the policy, the standards, the reference material, and the links — and none of your personal data.

  • No individual compliance tracking — certification status, CLP balances, and IDPs are personal records managed in CAMP/CAPPMIS with your supervisor
  • No course enrollment — training registration happens through DAU and your official channels
  • No login — everything on this page is general-purpose and openly readable

Roles & Responsibilities · Per the Policy

Who Does What

PEOs & Commanders

Nominate and Support

  • Exercise proactive succession planning for seamless ACMA transitions
  • Nominate ACMAs who meet the policy's qualification criteria
  • Submit the signed nomination to the Army DACM for review and approval
  • Enforce DACM standards and support the ACMA in their duties
Army DACM Office

Charter and Enable

  • Appoints an ACMA Program Manager working directly with ACMAs
  • Staffs nominations, orients new ACMAs, and issues signed charters
  • Holds at least one in-person and one virtual ACMA Summit each fiscal year
  • Sends monthly ACMA career-information emails and supports AAW data analysis
The ACMA

Advise and Advocate

  • Principal advisor to command leadership and the DACM on acquisition career matters
  • Keeps the PEO/Command informed on DAWIA certification posture and annual standards
  • Ensures town halls and org briefings include an AAW component
  • Coordinates the Lead OAP designation; attends Summits and selection/award boards
Designation & Tenure

Qualifying and Nominating an ACMA

Qualification Criteria

  • GO, SES, or GS-15/Colonel (or broadband equivalent) senior leader
  • Assigned to an acquisition-coded position — preferably a Critical Acquisition Position; non-acquisition-coded nominees require written command justification
  • Meets the DAWIA certification requirement for their current position
  • Has at least four years of acquisition experience

Tenure

The DACM signs a charter designating the ACMA for up to four years. At term end, the DACM Office and the command mutually decide on renewal or a new nomination. The DACM retains sole removal authority.

The Nomination Packet

Submitted electronically to the Army DACM Office's ACMA Program Manager:

  • Organization's ACMA nomination memorandum (template below)
  • Resume or biography
  • Command justification

Reference · The Dual-Hat Reality

Cyber Civilian + Acquisition Professional

Many Army civilian cybersecurity professionals carry two frameworks at once: DoD 8140 / DCWF qualification for the cyber work role, and DAWIA certification for the acquisition-coded billet. The general rules below are reference material — your individual status lives in CAMP/CAPPMIS and your 8140 record, not on this page.

Continuous Learning Points — The General Rule

Reference

Every AAW professional earns 80 CLPs per two-year cycle (goal: 40 per year). Common earning routes and typical values:

  • DAU courses and other training — 1 CLP per instruction hour
  • DAU credentials — varies by credential
  • College credit — 15 CLPs per semester hour, 10 per quarter hour
  • Conferences, symposia, teaching — 2 per hour (annual caps apply)
  • Mentoring, rotational assignments, publications, professional certifications — per the current standards memo

Exact values and caps are set annually — always defer to the current AAW Standards Memorandum on the USAASC Policy Library ⤤. Log activities in CAMP/CAPPMIS.

CAP & KLP — Additional Requirements

Statutory

Critical Acquisition Positions (CAP): at least four years of acquisition experience, DAWIA certification at the required tier (or within grace), a three-year tenure agreement, typically GS-14/O-5 and above.

Key Leadership Positions (KLP): deeper experience requirements, program-office time for ACAT I/II roles, and certification at the highest tier for the functional area — required at time of assignment, with tenure agreements to match.

Position coding questions go through your supervisor and the Lead OAP; requirements are set DoD-wide, not locally.

The Annual Battle Rhythm

Every FY
  • Start of FY: DACM signs the AAW Standards Memorandum — certification, CLP, IDP, SRPE, and ethics standards for the year
  • Through the year: IDP reviewed with your supervisor at least every 6 months; monthly ACMA career-information email
  • Summits: at least one in-person and one virtual ACMA Summit per FY
  • Cyber side: 8140 qualification and continuous-education requirements run in parallel — see the Workforce Development Center
