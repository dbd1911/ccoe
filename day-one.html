Where You Sit — the 30-Second Map

The Cyber Authority Chain

CISO sets policy & posture→ AODR reviews & recommends→ AO accepts risk & signs
ISSM governs the portfolio→ ISSO runs daily ops→ ISSE engineers the design

Decisions flow UP. Direction flows DOWN. Evidence flows UP continuously.

The Program Chain

PEO→ PM (owns the program)→ APM (deputy)
Product Mgr (owns an increment)→ Chief Engineer (technical authority)→ Lead PE (integration)

Cyber sits next to this chain, not above it: the ISSM/ISSO advise the PM; the ISSE advises the Chief Engineer. Risk decisions go up-chain — never sideways.

The one rule that never changes: if it wasn't recorded, it wasn't decided. Every approval, every acceptance, every risk decision lands in writing — that's not bureaucracy, that's your paper trail when you need it later.

The Logistics Nobody Volunteers

  • Know which network you're working on — NIPR vs enclave vs STN; the rules differ on each
  • Policies, templates, and this site live HERE — bookmark the hub on day one
  • Meet your counterpart on day 2: PM ↔ ISSM ↔ CHEN ↔ ISSE — the whole machine runs on those four relationships
  • Everything you sign, write it down; everything you decide, record it
  • Say "I'm new" early — every office has a go-to person who remembers being new

ISSM

Single security-manager role — owns the portfolio's security strategy and package routing.

 SP 800-160 · SECURE DESIGN

ISSE

Engineers security into the system from requirements to authorization.

Owns the program — cost/schedule/performance and its cyber posture. APM = the deputy lens.

Owns a capability increment — the backlog's security starts with you.

The PM's technical authority — architecture baseline and ARB discipline.

Runs day-to-day engineering across government + contractor teams.
