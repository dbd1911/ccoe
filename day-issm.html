Day One · Cyber Governance · DCWF 722

ISSM

There is one ISSM for the organization. You run the security governance chain for every system in the portfolio — strategy, routing, escalation — on the authorizing official’s behalf. Day-to-day RMF execution at the program level belongs to the ISSO cadre: the former PM-level ISSM billets were re-titled ISSO. Your job is to govern them, not to be them.

What Is My Job

The Charter — What the Army Expects of a ISSM

Split the way you'll actually use it: what you own outright, what you support, what you hand upward. When ownership is unclear, this page is the tiebreaker.

You Own
  • The authorization strategy for every system in the portfolio — one chain, one ISSM
  • Package routing decisions + readiness calls before the AODR sees anything
  • Workforce load balancing + escalation adjudication across the ISSO cadre
You Support
  • The AO/AODR chain — your packages are their raw material
  • Your ISSOs — including the former PM-level ISSMs, now executing at ISSO — you are their manager and their shield
You Escalate
  • Inter-system risk conflicts
  • Anything that would surprise the AODR — nothing should
One ISSM — know the re-titling: there is a single ISSM for the organization. The PM-level-and-below ISSM billets were re-titled ISSO — those billets execute RMF daily for their programs and roll up through you. If your billet was a PM-level ISSM before the re-title, you are now ISSO — your experience maps to the ISSO day-to-day lanes (see the ISSO page) plus the governance you keep here: strategy, routing, escalation, workforce balancing.

The Chain

You report to: the AO/AODR chain for posture matters; you advise the system owners (PM/PD)
Who works with you: the ISSO cadre — the former PM-level ISSMs, now re-titled ISSO — plus the ISSEs

Army Line

DA PAM 25-2-14 §3-12 is your charter — ISSMs are the technical advisors supporting the AO and the system owner, in charge of continuous monitoring and compliance across their purview. This portfolio runs the org-real model: one ISSM (governance + strategy), with the former PM-level ISSM billets re-titled ISSO (§3-15) executing RMF day to day for their programs. Single accountable manager per lane, no ambiguity.
