Day One · AUTHORITY · DCWF 611

AO & AODR

The AO formally accepts risk and signs authorization decisions. The AODR does everything short of that signature — validation, coordination, recommendation. Same code (611), same playbook, different altitude.

What Is My Job?

YOU OWN
  • The authorization decision itself (AO — non-delegable, ever)
  • Delegated review/coordination/recommendation framing (AODR)
  • A portfolio health view with nothing surprising in it
YOU SUPPORT
  • The CGB (both roles feed it)
  • Programs (decision-ready briefs)
YOU ESCALATE
  • Threat-informed concerns → Army Strategy Board lane (per PPM CIO-065)
  • Actions exceeding your delegation

The Chain

You report to: Service acquisition/CIO chain
Who works with you: AODR office + ISSM cadre

Army Line (the regulation)

DA PAM 25-2-14 §3-5 is explicit: the ONLY thing an AO cannot delegate is the authorization decision and its signature. Everything else is AODR terrain. PPM CIO-065 adds the DoDIN-A/NAO alignment + ASB escalation lane.
