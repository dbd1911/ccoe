Program & Engineering Leadership Roles

Program-Office cyber roles — who does what under the Adaptive Acquisition Framework, and the authoritative resources for each. Cyber works for the mission; these roles own the mission machinery that makes security executable.

DCWF 801 · Cyberspace Enablers

Program Manager (PM)

Owns the program: cost, schedule, performance, and the cybersecurity posture of every fielded capability. Designates the ISSM/ISSO structure, signs system-level decisions up the AAF chain, and answers to the PEO.

  • Army Acquisition Workforce — career model (USAASC)
  • DCWF 801 Program Manager — qualification pathways (DoD Cyber Exchange)
  • AR 70-1 — Operation of the Adaptive Acquisition Framework (governing regulation)
  • DoDI 8140.02 — cyberspace workforce requirements (PM-qualifiable roles)
  • CCoE: the 9-clause library → what cyber must be IN the contract
  • CCoE: CGB + authority model → which decisions the PM owns vs. delegates
DCWF 803/805 · Cyberspace Enablers

Product Manager & Product Owner

Owns a product line or capability increment end-to-end — the "why and what," balanced against risk. In Agile-delivered programs the PM/Product Owner is the cyber requirements' first stop: what gets built Secure-by-Design at the story level.

  • DCWF specialty: Acquisition & Program/Project Management (public.cyber.mil)
  • DoD 8140 Readiness Bulletin — current workforce qualification guidance
  • USAASC — acquisition leader development & talent management
  • DoDI 8140.02 (the workforce instruction)
  • CCoE: AI guardrails → permitted platforms for product decision support
DCWF 801 (support track)

Assistant Program Manager (APM)

Deputy to the PM across a functional slice — often the schedule/budget spine, test integration, or fielding. Frequently the cyber-requirement tracker of record for contract deliverables and POA&M funding alignment.

  • Army acquisition career development (USAASC)
  • DAU — Defense Acquisition University courses (ACQ/PQM credentialing)
  • AAF Pathway guides (DAU) — 6 pathways, cyber decision points
  • CCoE: AO Package Routing Checklist + Significant Change Request templates
  • CCoE: expiration calendar → the dates your program answers for
TECHNICAL AUTHORITY

Chief Engineer (CHEN)

The PM's senior technical authority — owns the system's engineering baseline, architecture review discipline, and technical risk posture. Works FOR the PM. Interfaces daily with the ISSE cadre on control inheritance, boundary architecture, and STIG-ability of every design decision.

  • NIST SP 800-160 Vol. 1 — Systems Security Engineering (the book of record)
  • AAF Middle Tier pathway — rapid fielding w/ cyber gates
  • DISA STIGs & SRGs — the configuration floor every design must meet
  • DoDI 8500.01 — the cybersecurity program the Chief Engineer implements inside
  • CCoE: 6 reference architectures that already passed + non-negotiables
DCWF 802 · PROJECT TRACK

Lead Project Engineer (LPE)

Runs the day-to-day engineering effort across contractor + government teams — the integration point where schedules meet technical reality. Tracks the cyber-relevant engineering artifacts (test evidence, STIG scans, pipeline attestations) through to delivery acceptance.

  • DCWF 802 IT Project Manager — qualification pathways
  • DAU career management — engineering & project tracks
  • SCAP tooling (SCC 1.3) — the scan artifacts your deliveries produce
  • CCoE: tool belt — ACAS, SCC, STIG Viewer walkthroughs
  • CCoE: inspection binder standard — how your artifacts get judged
